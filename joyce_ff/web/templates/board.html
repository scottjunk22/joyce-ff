How to read the board

All numbers are per game, in this league's threshold scoring (a 74-yard, no-TD game counts as 0). Hover any column header for the short version.
# — rank
Strict order (1, 2, 3…) by whatever column you're sorted on. No ties — someone is always ahead.
Tier — value groups
Players grouped between value cliffs. Same tier ≈ interchangeable; a tier drop is a real drop-off worth drafting ahead of. Three same-tier backs? Take whichever fills a need. Only one left before the next tier? That's the cliff — grab it.
Proj/g — projected points per game
What this asset scores in a typical week. Every real game (2023–25, recent seasons weighted heavier) was scored with the validated engine and averaged — threshold-aware, so weak games correctly count as 0. Shrunk toward replacement when the sample is thin.
VOR — value over replacement
Points/game above a freely available player at the same slot (≈ the 33rd-best RB / 44th-best receiver in your 11-team division draft; the 12th team for units). The real draft currency — and the one number that compares an RB vs a kicker vs a QB room on the same scale.
Floor — a typical bad week
25th-percentile game: 1 in 4 of his games land at or below this. The downside you can roughly count on.
Ceil — a typical good week
75th-percentile game: 1 in 4 games are at or above this. Floor↔Ceil width shows consistency: tight = steady, wide = boom-bust.
Bust% — how often he scores zero
Share of games that scored a flat 0. Under threshold scoring zeros are common, so a high bust% is a real cost even if the average looks fine.
G25 — games played in 2025
Sample-size / recency check. G25 = 17 is a full, trustworthy season; a high projection with G25 = 2 is thin evidence (shrunk down and flagged, never chased).
